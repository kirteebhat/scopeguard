# Agency Founder & Project Manager Customer Discovery Protocol

**Goal:** Understand how software development and creative agencies currently identify, negotiate, and bill out-of-scope work, and determine the willingness to pay for automated scope change approval.

**Methodology Guidelines (The Mom Test):**
- Inquire strictly about **past behavior and real historical projects**, never hypothetical future preferences.
- Do not pitch ScopeGuard during the interview; listen for actual friction points, workarounds, and financial write-offs.
- Do not invent or fabricate interview responses; record verbatim quotes and exact monetary amounts.

---

## The 10 Customer Discovery Interview Questions

### Section 1: Past Behavior & Baseline Workflow
1. **Can you walk me through the last time a client requested a feature or design change that was clearly outside the original statement of work?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* How was the request communicated (email, Slack, verbal Zoom call)? What was the very next step your team took before writing any code?
2. **Think back to your last completed fixed-price or milestone project. By how many days or weeks did the final delivery date differ from the initial contract deadline?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* What specific factor accounted for the majority of that schedule shift? Was the deadline officially revised in writing, or did it slip informally?

### Section 2: Frequency & Scope Creep Identification
3. **In an average client engagement, how often do scope deviations occur?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* At what point does your team classify a request as "billable extra work" versus "a minor courtesy tweak we absorb"?
4. **Who on your team is typically the first person to notice that a client request exceeds the agreed deliverable?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* How do your developers or designers report that to the account manager or client?

### Section 3: Financial Impact & Revenue Leakage
5. **Over the past 12 months, can you recall a specific instance where your agency ended up doing unbilled work because getting formal client sign-off felt too slow or awkward?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* How many developer hours were absorbed on that instance? What was the estimated monetary value of that unbilled time?
6. **Have you ever had a client dispute an invoice at the end of a project, claiming they never agreed to additional charges for a requested change?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* How was that dispute resolved? Did you discount the invoice, write off the hours, or have written proof they had authorized it?

### Section 4: Current Workarounds & Tooling
7. **What software tools, templates, or manual documents do you currently use to propose and track scope modifications?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* If you use documents (e.g. Google Docs, PDF change orders), how long does it take from the client's request to having a signed document in hand?
8. **When you send a change proposal to a client, how do they indicate their approval?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* Do they reply "looks good" in email, type a message in Slack, or use an electronic signature tool (like DocuSign/PandaDoc)?

### Section 5: Willingness to Pay & Commercial Value
9. **If you look at the total revenue your agency generated last year, what percentage would you estimate was lost to unbilled scope creep or disputed hours?**
   - *Probing Follow-up:* What tools or software do you currently pay for specifically to safeguard project margins or track contract baselines?
10. **Suppose a dedicated tool allowed your team to send a 1-click mobile approval link that instantly bound the client's sign-off to an immutable version snapshot. How much would your agency consider a reasonable monthly subscription for that capability?**
    - *Probing Follow-up:* Would that decision require leadership approval, or does a project manager have the budget discretion to expense ₹799–₹1,999/mo ($29–$79/mo) per project team?
